Today I am considering how artists will survive in ever expensive cities. I am currently lucky that I have time to devote to my craft. However, cities being our cultural hubs are becoming ever more expensive with artistic spaces being pushed out in favor of mind numbing corporate infrastructure and soulless housing blocks.

In Seattle, my home, I became used to our tech overlords expansion in burbs, while the city itself remained a refuge for culture. The final death blow for Seattle was the Amazon corporate campus, built smack dab in the middle of the city. It created a city within a city. A bubble of ultra wealthy individuals amongst the unhoused, the artists, the cafés, the clubs, the sports venues, the restaurants and everything else that embodied the culture of the city. Amazon employees in Seattle rarely interact with those outside of their corporate bubble. They have completely different incentives than actual residents of the city. A level 10 Cobalt programmer (ha) with plans to hibernate in a high-rise condo collecting $600k/year paychecks does not have the same long term interest in the livability of the city as a family of four that plans to live and work in the city for the rest of their lives do.

Why would a young professional with no ties to the city care about Seattle's homeless, when half their paycheck leaves the city and they have no plans to stay here. Why invest in the city if it is simply a career pitstop? The costs don't matter when lunches are catered, you pay a stylist to dress you, doordash is your version of dining and Ikea appears to have set up a pop up in your rental.

This K-shaped hellscape American cities are becoming is not the world I want to live in. I've been criticized as being unreasonable and "socialist," but I am just asking for soul. I am asking for cities to remain hubs of creative dynamism, not soul sucking AI slop factories. There will always be business in any city, but when tech campuses invade the heartbeat of the city, creating a private, ultra wealthy city within a city. That is not progress. It is hot trash.

I'd rather read a book than have a novel read to me. I'd rather drink coffee out of a hand built mug than some plastic monstrosity found at Whole Foods. I want live music, not Spotify. I want plugged in guitars, not AI generated beats.
